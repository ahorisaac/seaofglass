Threat, Vulnerability & Risk

12 min Reading Lab: pending Quiz: pending

Lesson complete — progress saved.

Threat is the storm. Vulnerability is the open window. Risk is what you might lose.

risk ≈ threat × vulnerability × impact

Beginners mix these three words constantly. Keep the window picture in your head and you’ll never confuse them again. Security work is mostly about finding windows, closing them, and deciding which storms you can live with.

T Threat — “the storm”

A potential cause of harm: a ransomware crew, a phishing campaign, a careless insider, a flood, a power cut. You usually can’t remove threats from the world — you reduce how often they succeed against you.

  • Ask: Who or what could hurt this system?
  • Examples: attacker, malware, natural disaster, human error.

V Vulnerability — “the open window”

A weakness a threat can exploit: an unpatched server, a weak password, an untrained team, an unlocked server room. Vulnerabilities are the part you can directly fix.

  • Ask: What weakness lets the threat in?
  • Examples: missing patch, default credentials, open S3 bucket, no MFA.

R Risk — “what you might lose”

The expected loss if a threat exploits a vulnerability. Risk blends likelihood and impact. You manage risk; you rarely reach zero. A low-value test server and a hospital database can share the same bug but carry very different risk.

  • Ask: If this goes wrong, how bad is it — and how likely?
  • Examples: data breach fines, downtime cost, reputational damage.
Worked example — phishing at a clinic

Threat pressure 7/10 (constant campaigns) · Exposure 8/10 (no training, no MFA) · Impact 9/10 (patient records). Add training and MFA and the numbers drop — the storm still exists, but fewer windows are open and the loss shrinks.

A critical risk: patient records could leak through staff inboxes.

What do you do with a risk?

Treatment Meaning Example
Avoid Stop the risky activity entirely. Shut down an old public FTP server.
Mitigate Add controls to lower likelihood or impact. Patch the server, add MFA, train staff.
Transfer Shift some loss to someone else. Cyber insurance, managed security provider.
Accept Document it and live with it. Low-risk legacy tool kept behind segmentation.

Memory hook

“Storm. Window. Loss.” Threat = storm, vulnerability = window, risk = loss. Controls don’t delete storms — they close windows and shrink losses.
