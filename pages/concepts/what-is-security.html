What is Security?

10 min Reading Lab: pending Quiz: pending

Lesson complete — progress saved.

Security isn’t a product you buy. It’s a promise you keep — while someone is actively trying to break it.

security = keeping promises under attack

Every system promises three things: secrets stay secret, data stays true, and systems stay usable. You’ll meet these as the CIA Triad. Security is the daily work of keeping those promises even when a clever human is trying to break them.

Why “under attack” matters: reliability handles accidents — storms, bugs, power cuts. Security assumes an adversary who adapts. A locked door stops rain forever. It stops a burglar only until they find the window.

The defender’s job, in one sentence Make attacks cost more than they’re worth — and notice them fast when they happen.

Four supporting promises

Authentication

You are who you claim to be.

Authorization

You may do only what you’re allowed to do.

Non-repudiation

You can’t deny what you signed or sent.

Privacy

You control how your data is used.

Five ideas you’ll use forever

  1. Security is a process, not a product. Patch, review, rehearse — forever. Attackers adapt; so must you.
  2. Defense in depth. One control will fail. Use layers, so one failure isn’t game over.
  3. Least privilege. Give every user, app and service only the access it needs — nothing more.
  4. Assume breach. Design so a stolen password doesn’t become a stolen company.
  5. The human is part of the system. Train and support people; don’t just blame them.

Lab: defense in depth

Spin the stack, spread the layers, and focus each one. Notice the order attackers meet them: People → Perimeter → Network → Host → Application → Data.

click a layer to focus it

Data. The crown jewels: encryption, access rules and tested backups. If every other layer fails, encrypted and backed-up data still keeps its promises.

Why you need this

Industry breach reports consistently find the same patterns: phishing and stolen credentials dominate initial access, unpatched known bugs keep causing mass incidents, and breaches are often discovered months after they begin. That’s why defenders need layers, least privilege and fast detection — not just a bigger firewall.
